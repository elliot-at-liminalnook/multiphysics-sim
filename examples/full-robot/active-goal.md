Build an end-to-end workflow for teaching this quadruped to walk using quadruped-appropriate PLANC-inspired planning references, teacher-policy reinforcement learning, student distillation, and training with bounded environmental perturbations.

A required outcome is a walking robot that we can control interactively in realtime in a shareable WASM browser application. Deliver this experience early with a baseline walking controller, then maintain it as planning and learned controllers improve. Do not defer browser walking until the detailed simulator reaches realtime or the learning pipeline is complete.

Keep CAD as the source of the robot's physical definition. Store geometry, materials, mass/inertia, joints, transmissions, actuators, sensors, contact properties, and limits with explicit units, coordinate frames, provenance, and uncertainty. Distinguish measured, derived, and estimated values. Record experimental overrides and provide a path to promote accepted values back into CAD.

Support explicitly versioned fidelity profiles derived from that definition:
- A detailed validation model that retains relevant physical behavior and supports calibration, mechanism investigation, actuator/load analysis, and comparison against hardware.
- A fast training model that preserves the behavior needed for useful planning and initial policy learning.
- A browser interaction model that may substantially simplify mechanisms, actuators, contact, compliance, electrical/thermal dynamics, and other expensive details to achieve realtime walking and responsive control. It may share the training profile where appropriate.

Make simplifications reusable Rust library capabilities, selected through explicit configuration. Examples include embedded or reduced linkage coordinates, effective transmissions, lumped inertias, bounded effective actuator response, and simpler contact geometry and laws. Preserve meaningful motor-to-foot motion, balance, actuator feasibility, and terrain interaction for the intended browser tasks. Document omitted effects, approximations, provenance, and operating limits. Do not present the simplified model as high-fidelity validation or replace live physics with animation to claim realtime.

Rust owns simulation, environments, and controllers; Python remains on the CAD side. Rhai scripts use shared registered Rust components. Keep robot, world, task, and policy separate. Native headless runs and WASM must use the same runtime implementation for each selected fidelity profile and compatible, versioned observation/action contracts. Do not duplicate physics in the viewer. Distinguish privileged teacher observations from deployable sensor observations, and make required controller/profile adaptations explicit.

Make realtime browser interaction a measured acceptance requirement. On documented reference hardware and browser, demonstrate sustained at least 1 simulated second per wall-clock second during representative walking, turning, stopping, and terrain contact, with a 50 Hz control interface and a target p95 control-transition processing time of at most 20 ms. Measure rendering responsiveness and command-to-visible-response latency separately. Report stalls, slowdowns, and accuracy tradeoffs honestly. Physics advances on simulation time independently of rendering, and expensive work runs off the UI thread. If the browser misses the target, simplify or optimize its profile and revalidate it; do not reduce the requirement to smooth playback of a slow simulation.

Maintain a polished browser viewer and interactor throughout development. Provide labeled model/controller/environment presets, clear fidelity and calibration status, responsive orbit/pan/zoom and component focus, follow views, pause/reset/single-step/replay, and WASD motion requests routed through the controller. Expose speed/turn commands, controller state, targets versus actual motion, contacts, relevant actuator telemetry, and simulation performance. Show clear loading, progress, cancellation, and error states. Distinguish live physics, reference animation, and recorded playback. Support reproducible run saving and easy video export. Package the WASM module and required assets so another person can interact with the robot without installing CAD.

Establish task-specific accuracy gates for each profile: motor-to-foot kinematics, foot placement and clearance margins, loaded actuator tracking, balance, stepping success, contact/slip, disturbance recovery, and timestep sensitivity. Compare whole trajectories and task outcomes across profiles and against refined references. A fast browser profile can have wider, explicitly justified tolerances than the detailed model. Calibrate against available hardware measurements, and provide reproducible measurement procedures and data import for missing evidence. Keep unmeasured parameters and unverified transfer claims explicit.

Implement reproducible resets, seeded environments, held actions, typed observations, rewards, termination/truncation, and replay. Progress through a controllable baseline gait, quadruped planning references, reference tracking, teacher-policy learning, student initialization/distillation, and robustness training. Begin initial learning once its task-specific simulation gates pass; do not require a near-exact digital twin first. Ground randomization and disturbances in plausible physical uncertainty and evaluate on held-out conditions. Use detailed-model comparisons and physical results to identify where additional fidelity matters.

Every controller milestone must remain runnable and inspectable in the browser. Evaluate controllers across their supported fidelity profiles, recording adaptations and failures rather than assuming transfer. Keep realtime browser checks, native/WASM parity, representative physics and controller tests, and replay checks alongside controller changes in CI. A controller milestone is not delivered while its browser experience is missing or broken.

Deliver versioned or durably referenced CAD artifacts, model/controller/task configurations, reproducible examples, benchmark evidence, and an easily shareable browser build. Follow AGENTS.md and contribute reusable functionality to the shared library before adding robot-specific code. Completion requires demonstrated planning, teacher-to-student initial learned control and robustness evaluation, plus a usable browser application with realtime walking and interactive WASD control. Animation alone, a faster isolated solver, or a working small fixture does not complete the goal.

Explain progress as self-contained lessons: what was tested, why it matters, what the evidence establishes, what remains uncertain, and what comes next.
