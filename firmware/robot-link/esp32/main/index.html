Hold a ◄ ► button to move; let go to stop. The drive ramps up and down smoothly, slows near the ends of the motor's travel and stops at them. If the Wi-Fi drops the hold for a moment, the motor carries on at the same drive for up to 0.4 s, then ramps down. A servo must be armed and in PWM mode.

Limits the board enforces (tighter than the FPGA's 60 °C and 9.0–12.6 V)

Each slot is a motor of the CAD robot: a leg and its hip swing (belt), worm (thigh) or foot slide. Put the servo that sits there in the slot by ID; a different motor in a slot starts untaught. On the leg's FPGA image a motor arms only inside its taught travel: Arm (in Servos) sends that window, then hold ◄ ► to move; a motor outside its travel is armed to come back in only. Poses are named as in the Mac's calibration panel. To move a pose: Open it, jog to the new place while watching, then Set it. Align saves the motor's reading at the CAD pose drawn for its joint. Everything here is kept on the board; the Mac pulls it into the repository (leg_gait_pack … --pull-robot), and gaits wait for poses that differ from the stored pack.

Drive

Hold W/S to walk forward or back, A/D to turn in place, W+A and so on to arc; the keys above work by touch. The robot keeps its step and blends into the held keys' gait over half a cycle. Let go and it steps in place, then stops at the start of a cycle and holds. Leaving the page, another window taking focus, or Space stops it with torque off.

The FPGA supervisor owns every safety promise: temperature, voltage and current trips, the dock's S2 button, its telemetry and command leases and, on the calibration profile, the taught travel windows. This page renews its session every 300 ms and a hold every 80 ms; the firmware carries a drive on through a gap of up to 400 ms after the last hold, then ramps it down and stops everything, a playing gait included, 1 s after the last sign of the page. Closing this tab, hiding it or leaving it stops the robot.
