# Reversal-aware crawl

The next browser controller is generated by `prepare_reversal.mjs` into
`browser-reversal/`. It retains the CAD geometry, mass, constraints, contact law
and effective actuators. Its changes are controller references and gains.

## What changed and why

A direction request previously took effect at each foot transfer. If a request
arrived after the first side foot had started moving, one side foot could finish
a forward step while the other took a reverse step. That staggered stance left
one support unloaded when the rear foot lifted. The shared `StepSequence` now
optionally restarts the configured foot order after a translational reversal,
finishing the current transfer first. A separate order cursor preserves the total
completed-transfer count. Planted references never move at the restart boundary.

Timing sweeps also caught a reference collision between the front thigh gear and
its housing-side pulley. The reverse posture's backward body shift was reduced
from 20 to 18 mm. This preserves support while leaving clearance; no motor or
collision limit was enlarged. The previous 0.5 N planning screen and actual
1 N support requirements remain distinct and unchanged.

A 60 s forward run completed 28 supported swings but missed the provisional
1 mm final body-position target at 1.25 mm. Increasing body feedback throughout
walking improved that offset but made one turning swing fail its nonlinear solve.
The accepted policy keeps the moving gain at 0.25. After a zero motion request,
it adds up to 0.5 only as all four feet carry load, reaching full extra gain when
every foot carries at least 5 N. This reduces final standing error without using
the stronger correction during an unloaded swing. These force observations are
privileged simulator signals; they are not claims about installed hardware sensors.

## Reproduce the checks

```sh
node examples/full-robot/prepare_reversal.mjs
cargo build --release -p sim-runtime --example run_environment --example evaluate_lift
target/release/examples/run_environment \
  examples/full-robot/browser-reversal/scene.json \
  examples/full-robot/browser-reversal/short.config.json \
  examples/full-robot/browser-reversal/task.json \
  examples/full-robot/browser-reversal/forward-reverse.actions.json > runs/reversal.json
node examples/full-robot/check_online_steps.mjs runs/reversal.json runs/reversal-check
node examples/full-robot/check_lift_batch.mjs runs/reversal.json runs/reversal-check
```

The short recipe is 24 s; the browser and sustained recipes are 60 s. The supplied
request schedules cover both reversal directions, reversals at 2.4/4.4/6.4 s,
forward/turn/reverse/stop, and 56 s forward followed by stopping. Use `config.json`
for the two 60 s schedules. `short-refined.config.json` halves the physics step
while keeping the controller at 50 Hz and preserving numerical tolerances; it
allows 80 Newton iterations, as the earlier refinement recipe required.

The checks still require at least 200 ms of simultaneous 1 mm clearance,
swing load at most 0.1 N and each support load at least 1 N for every completed
swing. Final body error must stay within 1 mm, tilt within 0.01 rad, final yaw
error within 0.005 rad, and sampled internal contacts must remain absent.
Both nominal and refined trajectories are checked. These are sampled flat-floor
commissioning gates, not hardware transfer or between-sample collision guarantees.

## Faster validation without different criteria

`evaluate_lift` now accepts an array of requirements as well as a single object.
The batch path constructs the model and reads the recording once, computes each
required foot's surface clearance once per frame, then uses the unchanged shared
lift evaluator for each window. A batch emits `reports` and reusable
`sample_series`; the single-window output remains compatible.

The checker verifies equivalence against single-window calls, including repeated
swing links and an intentionally impossible 100 m clearance requirement. The
impossible requirement still fails. This removes repeated parsing and geometry
preparation from the experiment loop without changing acceptance thresholds.

## Measured browser checkpoint

`reversal-status.json` preserves capture hashes and the measured outcomes. Both
24 s reversal directions and switching at 2.4/4.4/6.4 s pass every swing check.
The 60 s turn/reverse case passes ten swings; sustained forward passes 28, advances
69.55 mm and stops with 0.965 mm body-position error. Halving the physics step
in the 24 s reversal case changes feet by at most 0.479 mm and the body by
0.221 mm, within the existing 1/0.5 mm comparison budgets.

The 3,000-transition turn/reverse native/WASM comparison passes with maximum
numeric difference 1.78e-10 and exact same-host replay/reset. The real viewer's
sustained-forward keyboard recording exactly matches the accepted native recipe,
seed, command events, completed steps and task. Browser UI checks pass, including
record/replay/reset, WASD release and focus loss, and narrow layout.

On the Intel i9-9980HK Mac with Chrome 152.0.7977.76 and Intel UHD 630 through
ANGLE Metal, the WebGL-enabled headless viewer completes 60 simulated seconds in
59.996 s. The 56 active seconds take 55.948 s. Whole/active p95 worker round-trip
latencies are 28.47/28.99 ms: **the 20 ms target still fails**. rAF scheduling p95
is 16.67 ms; this does not measure monitor presentation or command-to-visible
response. The approximately 70 mm advance is a slow commissioning crawl, not
useful normal walking speed. Terrain and broader teleoperation remain open.

## Limits

The measured cases do not establish arbitrary command switching, uneven-terrain
walking, disturbance recovery, calibrated hardware behavior or learned control.
Browser performance must be measured during active walking as well as over the
whole episode; standing time must not hide expensive transitions. The detailed
model remains available. The full planning, teacher/student learning and sim-to-real
workflow is still unfinished.
