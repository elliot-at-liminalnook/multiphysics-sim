Vertical slice implementation plan — v0.1

Controller to a visible linear actuator.

A self-contained plan for building and testing one complete machine in Rust: a sampled position controller commands a motor driver, a DC motor turns a reduction gear and lead screw, and a constrained carriage moves in the 3D scene. It is deliberately narrow, but it exercises every important seam in the larger multi-domain architecture.

1. The outcome we are building toward

The demo opens on a small electromechanical assembly. Changing a position target causes the controller to command the H-bridge, the motor current and torque to rise, the gears and lead screw to rotate, and the carriage to extend. Load, voltage, and end-stop changes visibly alter the motion. The same state values drive the scene, plots, inspector, and automated assertions.

Input

Desired carriage position

A causal signal in metres, changed by a scenario or UI control.

Physical result

Coupled motion and load

Voltage creates current, current creates torque, and torque becomes linear force.

Visible result

One truthful 3D view

Rotor, gears, screw, and carriage project from committed solver state.

How this follows the global architecture: this is a vertical slice, not a one-off demo. It proves directed control signals, typed acausal connectors, cross-domain bridge behaviors, transactional state, a coupling-island solver, and one-way 3D projection in one inspectable machine.

2. The five ideas a non-physicist needs

Signals carry information

The target, measured position, and duty command have a direction: a sender computes a value and a receiver reads it. These are ordinary causal data-flow edges.

Connectors carry interaction

A wire, shaft, or sliding joint does not have a natural input and output. Both attached parts jointly determine voltage/current, angle/torque, or position/force. The compiler solves those relationships together.

Behavior is separate from identity

The motor is one selectable ObjectId, but it owns electrical, rotational, and visual behaviors. Ports and state have their own stable IDs so nothing depends on array order or a display name.

State is the truth

Current, angles, velocity, controller integral, and carriage position live in the StateStore. Solver arrays and render transforms are disposable views of those stable values.

A step is a transaction

The solver experiments on trial state until its equations agree. Only a successful step becomes committed state; failure leaves the visible machine and controller unchanged.

Geometry is a view, not physics

A glTF mesh says what the assembly looks like. Behaviors say how it acts. Visual bindings place meshes from solved angles and positions, and the solver never reads the resulting transforms back.

The motor case, gearbox case, screw bearing, and rail are attached to a fixed chassis boundary. Those boundaries impose zero motion but still report reaction torque and force. That matters: “fixed” must mean the ground absorbs the equal-and-opposite load, not that the load silently disappears.

Architecture fit: the DC motor and lead screw are bridge behaviors. Each explicitly owns ports from the domains it couples; no domain crate reaches into another domain’s state. The connected plant becomes one strongly coupled island because electrical current, shaft speed, and carriage force affect one another within the same step.

4. Committed modeling choices

These choices make the first implementation understandable and deterministic while keeping the seams needed for later fidelity.

Time

50 μs fixed plant step

The electro-mechanical island advances at 20 kHz. A fixed step removes event-location and rollback complexity from the first slice while resolving the motor’s electrical time constant.

Control

1 kHz sampled PI controller

It runs exactly every 20 plant steps. Its duty output is held between samples, making discrete timing explicit and repeatable.

Power electronics

Averaged H-bridge

It applies average motor voltage instead of simulating every transistor edge. This preserves bus voltage, duty, current limit, reversal, and power loss without requiring nanosecond switching steps.

Mechanics

One-dimensional axes first

Rotational and translational connectors model the power path. Full six-degree-of-freedom bodies and general contact are unnecessary to prove the coupling or render the assembly.

Numerics

Implicit midpoint plus Newton

The method solves electrical and mechanical state together and behaves well for the stiff mix of small inductance, heavy carriage, and end-stop compliance.

Contact

Compliant end stop

A spring-damper force activates beyond the allowed stroke. It is deterministic and differentiable enough for Newton; general collision/contact remains a later domain.

Geometry

glTF or procedural meshes

Named assembly nodes are bound to stable state IDs. STEP import and automatic joint inference are not prerequisites for validating the runtime.

Determinism

Same machine, same result

Stable iteration order, fixed timing, and explicit event scheduling make regression traces repeatable on one machine. Cross-platform bit identity is not required.

Intentional boundary: the first H-bridge is an averaged behavioral model, not a transistor-level circuit. Its external electrical contract stays the same, so a switching model can replace it later when ripple, EMI, or switching losses become the question.

5. The physics, in software-developer language

Each equation is a small contract contributed by one behavior. The compiler combines them with connection equations; the solver finds values that satisfy all contracts simultaneously.

V = R·i + L·di/dt + Kₑ·ω

Motor electrical balance. Applied voltage is spent pushing current through resistance, changing magnetic energy in the winding, and opposing the voltage generated by the spinning motor. That last term is why a faster motor naturally draws less current at the same voltage.

τ = Kₜ·i

Motor conversion. Current produces torque. In SI units Kₜ and Kₑ have matching numerical values for an ideal motor; together the two equations conserve power apart from winding loss.

J·dω/dt = Στ

Rotational motion. Net torque changes angular speed. J is rotational inertia—the rotational analogue of mass. Angle then accumulates from speed through dθ/dt = ω.

θout = θin/N

Ideal reduction gear. A ratio N = 10 makes the output turn ten times slower and ideally produce ten times the torque. The paired torque equation includes efficiency and the sign convention required for power balance.

x = k·θ,   k = lead/(2π)

Lead screw conversion. One screw revolution advances the nut by one lead. The force/torque constraint is τ + k·F = 0 in the lossless sign convention, so rotational power τ·ω matches linear power F·v.

m·dv/dt = ΣF

Linear motion. Net force changes carriage velocity, and dx/dt = v updates position. Rail drag contributes −b·v; gravity is absent because the demo rail is horizontal.

Fstop = −ks·δ − cs·v

End-stop contact. Once the carriage penetrates a configured limit by δ, a stiff spring pushes it back and a damper removes chatter. This approximates local contact, not material deformation.

At a connection, across values agree and through values sum to zero. These two generic rules are the acausal graph’s core algorithm; each connector kind supplies its own units and power diagnostic.

6. Rust workspace and dependency direction

The workspace mirrors the global architecture. The slice implements only the necessary behavior descriptors, but it does so in the crates where future domains will live.

sim-test depends on the headless crates and owns reusable scenarios.

sim-core depends on bevy_ecs, slotmap, serde, and a units crate such as uom; it does not depend on rendering. sim-compile can use petgraph for connected components and strongly connected components. sim-solve starts with dense nalgebra solves because the demo island is small, while retaining a sparse Jacobian pattern for later replacement. sim-app alone depends on full bevy and a glTF loader.

Architecture fit: domain crates depend on core contracts, not on one another. The bridge crate imports connector schemas from the domains it joins. The app is at the top of the dependency graph, so the same scenario can run headlessly in CI.

7. Fundamental data structures

The editable graph is identity-rich and contains no solver-specific row numbers. Compilation creates dense, disposable layouts that map back to stable IDs.

struct ModelWorld {
    objects: SlotMap<ObjectId, SimObject>,
    behaviors: SlotMap<BehaviorId, BehaviorInstance>,
    ports: SlotMap<PortId, Port>,
    connections: Vec<Connection>,
    state: StateStore,
}

struct BehaviorInstance {
    object: ObjectId,       // the selectable physical/CAD identity
    kind: BehaviorTypeId,   // resolved through the plugin registry
    parameters: ParameterMap,
    state: Vec<StateId>,
}

enum PortSchema {
    Acausal(ConnectorTypeId),
    SignalIn(QuantityType),
    SignalOut(QuantityType),
}
trait BehaviorDescriptor: Send + Sync {
    fn declare(&self) -> BehaviorSchema;
    fn emit_residuals(&self, ctx: &mut ResidualBuilder);
    fn emit_jacobian(&self, ctx: &mut JacobianBuilder);
}

struct CompiledIsland {
    state_layout: DenseStateLayout,       // StateId ↔ dense index
    algebraic_layout: AlgebraicLayout,
    residual_ops: Vec<ResidualOp>,
    jacobian_pattern: CsrPattern,
    integrator: IntegratorKind,
}

struct VisualBinding {
    object: ObjectId,
    source: StateId,
    motion: VisualMotion,                 // rotate or translate on a local axis
}

The registry owns connector and behavior schemas, serialization hooks, state declarations, and residual builders. The compiler only asks descriptors what they contribute; it never contains a closed match over every motor, gear, or actuator type.

8. From graph to one accepted simulation step

Compile once after an edit

  1. Freeze an immutable snapshot of objects, behaviors, ports, parameters, connections, and stable state IDs.
  2. Validate signal direction, quantity units, connector types, and required ports.
  3. Collapse connected acausal ports into nodes; emit across-equality and through-conservation equations.
  4. Ask every behavior descriptor to emit residual equations and Jacobian entries.
  5. Match equations to unknowns and reject under- or over-constrained models with IDs a user can inspect.
  6. Partition the dependency graph into strongly connected coupling islands.
  7. Allocate dense trial arrays and retain StateId-to-index maps for state, rendering, traces, and diagnostics.

For this machine, the electrical, rotational, and translational equations form one plant island. The sampled controller is scheduled as a discrete causal event whose output is held constant while that island solves.

Advance one fixed step

const DT: Duration = Duration::from_micros(50);

loop {
    let mut trial = state.begin_trial();

    // Exactly every 20 plant steps, update the 1 kHz controller.
    scheduler.apply_due_events(time, &mut trial)?;

    // Newton may evaluate these equations repeatedly, but only against trial state.
    solve_implicit_midpoint(&compiled.plant_island, time, DT, &mut trial)?;

    // All state becomes visible together—or none of it does.
    state.commit(trial)?;
    observables.publish(state.committed());
    time += DT;
}

Implicit midpoint evaluates each dynamic equation halfway between old and proposed new state. Newton’s method repeatedly computes a residual vector R, solves J·Δx = −R, and applies Δx until every scaled residual is below tolerance. A line search prevents a large update from making a nonlinear saturation or end stop worse.

Architecture fit: trial/commit gives the controller integral, physical state, and any opaque block memory one mutation boundary. Repeated Newton evaluation is pure. A failed solve discards the entire trial, so the renderer never displays half of a step.

9. Making the same state visible in 3D

The scene has a chassis root plus nodes for motor rotor, input gear, output gear, screw, and carriage. Their rest transforms come from glTF or a procedural fixture. Their motion comes from visual bindings to committed state:

fn project_visuals(
    committed: Res<CommittedState>,
    mut visuals: Query<(&VisualBinding, &RestTransform, &mut Transform)>,
) {
    for (binding, rest, mut transform) in &mut visuals {
        *transform = match binding.motion {
            VisualMotion::Rotate { axis } =>
                rest.0 * rotation(axis, committed.angle(binding.source)),
            VisualMotion::Translate { axis } =>
                rest.0 * translation(axis * committed.length(binding.source)),
        };
    }
}

The output gear and screw may share a solved angle but have separate VisualBindings because their mesh origins differ. The carriage binds directly to its solved position. Render interpolation may blend between the previous and current commits for smoothness, but it may not invent or feed back physical state.

Minimum useful inspector

  • Selecting any mesh resolves its ObjectId and lists attached behaviors and ports.
  • Live values show target, measured position, duty, bus voltage, motor current, motor torque, shaft speed, carriage position, velocity, force, and mount reactions.
  • A time plot overlays target and position, with current and duty on separate axes.
  • Solver diagnostics show Newton iterations, residual norm, and any current-limit or end-stop activation.

Architecture fit: tests, traces, inspector, and renderer all resolve the same stable IDs from the committed StateStore. There is no second “animation state” to synchronize.

10. Implementation milestones

Each milestone ends in a runnable or inspectable proof. We avoid building a broad framework whose first end-to-end result appears months later.

  1. Workspace and deterministic scenario harness

    Create the crates, stable IDs, typed quantities, test clock, trace recorder, and a headless scenario runner.

    Exit: a scheduled scalar source produces byte-for-byte identical traces across repeated same-machine runs.

  2. Registry, connectors, and transactional state

    Implement behavior/connector descriptors, electrical/rotational/translational schemas, graph editing, trial state, commit, and rollback.

    Exit: invalid units and connector kinds fail with stable object/port IDs; rollback leaves all committed values unchanged.

  3. Compiler and small-island solver

    Emit connection and behavior residuals, create layouts and Jacobian patterns, partition islands, then add implicit midpoint, Newton, scaling, and diagnostics.

    Exit: an RL circuit and a mass-spring-damper match analytic/reference traces and show second-order convergence as the step halves.

  4. Open-loop motor

    Add the supply, averaged H-bridge, DC motor, rotor inertia, and fixed case mount. Drive a duty step without a controller.

    Exit: starting current, no-load speed, reversal, and electrical/rotational/loss power balance stay within declared tolerances.

  5. Gear, lead screw, carriage, and guide

    Add the ideal gear, rigid shaft connection, lead screw bridge, linear mass, damping, mount reactions, and compliant stroke limits.

    Exit: gear ratio and x = lead·θ/(2π) hold numerically, while force reflected back through the screw slows the motor.

  6. Closed-loop controller and driver limits

    Add the 1 kHz PI event, sample-and-hold, position sensor, duty saturation, current limiting, and anti-windup.

    Exit: the actuator reaches a commanded position, reverses, and recovers from saturation without integral runaway.

  7. 3D assembly and inspection

    Add a simple glTF/procedural assembly, visual bindings, state interpolation, selection, live values, and traces.

    Exit: captured transforms agree with state-derived transforms, and no render system has write access to physical state.

  8. Acceptance scenarios and reference artifacts

    Commit scenario files, trace baselines with justified tolerances, energy reports, and the obstruction demo used as the release gate.

    Exit: the full definition of done below passes headlessly and in the interactive app.

11. Test strategy

Fast

Unit and property tests

Check signs, units, local residuals, Jacobians against finite differences, connector conservation, PI saturation, and state transactions.

Structural

Compiler tests

Golden small graphs cover missing equations, extra constraints, incompatible ports, island partitioning, and stable-to-dense mappings.

Behavioral

Scenario tests

Drive the whole loop, record named observables, and assert outcomes across electrical, rotational, translational, and visual layers.

Numerical checks

  • Residual: every accepted step meets scaled absolute and relative tolerances.
  • Convergence: halving dt from 100 μs to 50 μs to 25 μs approaches a reference with the expected midpoint trend away from discontinuities.
  • Power: input electrical energy equals stored kinetic/magnetic energy, useful work, and declared loss within integrated tolerance.
  • Constraints: gear angle ratio and screw displacement relation remain below explicit error bounds.
  • Transactions: injected Newton failure does not advance PI integral, physical state, traces, or visual snapshots.
  • Visual projection: for sampled commits, scene transforms equal pure transforms calculated from the bound StateIds.

Testing tools

Use Rust’s built-in test runner plus proptest for connection and transaction invariants, insta only for readable compiler diagnostics, approx for dimensioned numerical comparisons, and criterion for compile/step benchmarks. Store reference traces as compact versioned data with the scenario parameters and solver version beside them; do not approve unexplained snapshot drift.

13. Definition of done

The slice is complete when a fresh developer can run one command for the headless suite and one for the app, select the actuator, command 120 mm with an obstruction at 80 mm, and observe all of the following:

  • The controller samples at exactly 1 kHz while the plant advances at exactly 50 μs.
  • The H-bridge reaches its 8 A limit; motor torque rises from that solved current.
  • The gear and lead-screw constraints convert motor rotation into the correct carriage position.
  • The obstruction stops the carriage, produces a mount reaction, and does not allow PI integral windup.
  • Releasing the obstruction resumes motion toward the target without an unstable jump.
  • Rotor, gears, screw, and carriage display transforms derive from the same committed IDs used by the assertions.
  • Connector power and whole-system energy reports close within declared numerical tolerance.
  • Repeated same-machine runs produce the same trace, and an injected failed step produces no partial mutation.

This one scenario proves the fundamental architecture: causal control can close a loop through an acausal multi-domain plant; bridge behaviors transfer power without cross-domain state access; constraints create reaction loads; state commits atomically; and 3D is a truthful projection of the solved machine.

What comes next, but not before this passes: replace the averaged bridge with a switching subsystem, introduce flexible screw or housing modes, bind imported CAD through the neutral assembly manifest, or replace the 1D guide with a 6-DOF rigid-body/contact domain. Each is a higher-fidelity behavior behind an interface already exercised here.

This document is the implementation companion to the global simulator architecture. Where it narrows a choice, the choice applies to this vertical slice rather than closing off later domain implementations.
