## hook: Too hot to touch
[[scroll top]] Hold a robot's leg still for a minute, and its servos can get too hot to touch, <short pause> though nothing is moving. Let's see where that heat comes from.

## joule: Torque costs heat
[[scroll block:torque-costs-heat]] [[highlight "the heat grows with the"]] Current through the winding makes heat. And torque needs current. <short pause> So the heat grows with the square of the torque. [[highlight off]]
Twice the torque, four times the heat. [[quiz heat]]

## where: Where the heat goes
[[scroll block:where-the-heat-goes]] [[box figure:thermal "heat in, heat out"]] Some heat warms the winding itself. The rest flows out to the air, faster the hotter it gets. [[unmark]]
It settles when all the heat made flows out. [[quiz final-rise]]

## fast: How fast
[[scroll block:how-fast-it-heats]] [[highlight "thermal time constant"]] How long it takes is set by the thermal time constant: heat capacity over conductance. For our motor, one minute. [[highlight off]]
Sketch the climb before we watch. [[quiz sketch-heat]]
[[scene five-minutes]] [[scroll scene:five-minutes]] [[play]] Five minutes, <short pause> fast forward. It climbs steeply, then bends over. [[wait-scene]] And it ends hotter than the simple estimate.

## hot: Hot copper
[[scroll block:hot-copper-makes-more-heat]] Why hotter? [[highlight "Copper's resistance rises about"]] Hot copper resists more, so the same current makes more heat. [[highlight off]] [[quiz hotter]]

## ratings: Continuous and peak
[[scroll block:continuous-and-peak]] [[highlight "continuous rating"]] The continuous rating is set by the cooling. [[highlight off]] <short pause> But for short bursts, the heat capacity soaks it up, so peaks can go much higher. [[quiz stall-time]]
