## hook: A lift
[[scroll top]] Lifts, grippers, printer axes. <short pause> They all turn a motor's rotation into straight travel. Let's lift two kilograms with our small motor.

## ratio: Every drive is a ratio
[[scroll block:every-linear-drive-is-a-ratio]] [[highlight "a fixed number of radians buys a metre of travel"]] With a belt or a screw, a fixed number of radians buys a metre of travel. [[highlight off]]
Call it the linear ratio. It's just a gear ratio, in radians per metre. The speed is divided by it. [[quiz g-screw]]

## force: Force goes the other way
[[scroll block:force-goes-the-other-way]] [[box figure:drives "belt and screw"]] And like a gear, it multiplies the torque into force, less the friction. [[unmark]]
[[scroll quiz:predict-belt]] So, can the belt lift two kilograms? [[quiz predict-belt]]
[[scene belt-lift]] [[scroll scene:belt-lift]] [[play]] [[wait-scene]] Eleven newtons up, <short pause> nearly twenty down. The load wins, and spins the motor backwards.

## screw: The screw
[[scroll block:the-screw]] Now a lead screw: five times the ratio. [[quiz predict-speed]]
[[scene screw-lift]] [[scroll scene:screw-lift]] [[play-until 0.78]] Both screws lift it. The fine one is slower, but stronger.
[[play]] [[wait-scene]] Then the power goes off. <short pause> The coarse thread runs back down. The fine thread holds. [[quiz why-hold]]

## choose: Choosing
[[scroll block:choosing-a-drive]] So: belts for fast, efficient, horizontal axes. <short pause> Fine screws for strong, slow lifts that must hold without power.
[[scroll block:design-the-lift]] Now design one yourself: a lift that reaches the top in time, and holds when the power goes off.

[[scroll block:putting-it-in-your-own-words]] Last step. Choose a drive for a lift and for a camera axis, and say why.
